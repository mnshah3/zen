  "policy_support": "The decisive event is legislative, and it happened in December 2025. The SHANTI Act 2025 (Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India) passed both Houses of Parliament on 18 December 2025 and received Presidential assent on 22 December 2025. It repeals and replaces BOTH the Atomic Energy Act 1962 and the Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act 2010, consolidating them into a single framework.\n\nWhat it permits private companies to do (via licence from the Central Government, separate licence per activity class, under Section 3 which extends eligibility to any company incorporated under the Companies Act 2013): build, own, operate and decommission nuclear reactors; fabricate nuclear fuel including conversion and refining and enrichment of U-235 up to notified thresholds; transport and store fuel and spent fuel; and import/export nuclear fuel, prescribed equipment, technology and software.\n\nWhat remains government-only: enrichment and isotopic separation (unless notified otherwise), spent fuel management including reprocessing and recycling and high-level waste, and heavy water production and upgradation.\n\nON THE LIABILITY QUESTION SPECIFICALLY \u2014 the constraint the investor's brief asks about is largely gone. The old CLNDA Section 17(b), which gave operators a statutory right of recourse against equipment suppliers and which single-handedly froze foreign vendor participation for fifteen years, does not survive. Under SHANTI Section 16, recourse exists only where expressly provided in a written contract, or where the incident results from a deliberate wrongful act by a third party \u2014 i.e. contractual, negotiable, and aligned with the international Convention on Supplementary Compensation norm that liability channels to the operator.\n\nOperator liability is now capped by reactor thermal rating: above 3,600 MWt, \u20b93,000 crore (~$334m); 1,500\u20133,600 MWt, \u20b91,500 crore (~$167m); 750\u20131,500 MWt, \u20b9750 crore (~$83.5m); 150\u2013750 MWt, \u20b9300 crore (~$33.4m); at or below 150 MWt, \u20b9100 crore (~$11.1m). Above the cap, the Central Government bears the liability under Section 14. Disputes route through an Atomic Energy Redressal Advisory Council and then the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity. Penalties for violations range \u20b950,000 to \u20b91 crore.\n\nNote the SMR-specific effect: the tiered cap means a 200 MWe Bharat Small Reactor sits in a low liability band, materially improving SMR project economics relative to a single large unit. This is a deliberate design choice, not an accident.\n\nThe residual constraint is equity, not liability: FDI in atomic energy remains prohibited, reconfirmed by the DAE on 10 December 2025, and the SHANTI Act makes no provision for foreign stakeholders. The Act's passage on 18 December 2025 and assent on 22 December 2025 was the most heavily telegraphed legislative event in the Indian power sector in years \u2014 it was trailed through 2025 in Business Standard and elsewhere, debated publicly, and covered by every law firm and vendor including Holtec's own promotional release. Treat any of these five facts as background, not signal.\n\nWhat is NOT yet priced, and is where a filings detector earns its keep: the Act creates a licensing regime that as of this research had granted nothing. The gap between \"private companies may now build reactors\" and \"this named company has a licence, a site, and an order\" is where the Sterlite-type re-rating event lives. The detectable, dateable events still ahead are (a) the first SHANTI Act licences actually issued and to whom, (b) NPCIL site allocations converting the 16 prospective BSR sites into awarded ones, (c) first hard orders for BSR long-lead components, (d) any resolution of the FDI prohibition, which would unlock Jaitapur and Kovvada and is a genuine binary, and (e) UCIL expansion contracts, which would be the first real evidence the uranium gap is being addressed rather than merely reported on.\n\nTwo cautions against the bull case. First, the fee-earning layer is not the scarce layer: the actual scarcity is uranium and enrichment, both closed to listed capital, so investors are buying the EPC and forging layer as a proxy for a shortage they cannot own.
