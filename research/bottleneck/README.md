# Bottleneck research

Sector research testing the investor's thesis that bottlenecks exist where
India depends on external suppliers. Each theme gets a researcher and an
independent sceptic whose job is to kill it.

Nine of ten themes were cut short by a session limit and are queued to re-run.
Nuclear completed, and its verdict is the most useful thing here so far.

| File | What it is |
|------|------------|
| `nuclear-RESEARCH.md` | the case for nuclear as a bottleneck |
| `nuclear-CHALLENGE.md` | the sceptic's attack on that case |
| `DETECTOR-SPEC.md` | filing-detection patterns derived from what survived |

## The nuclear verdict, in one line

The bottleneck and the opportunity do not overlap.

Where India genuinely depends on imports -- uranium, enrichment, heavy water --
the SHANTI Act 2025 reserves the activity to the state, so there is no listed
instrument that gets long the shortage. Where private capital IS allowed --
forgings, calandrias, steam generators, turbine islands -- India is already
self-sufficient, so no import gap exists to close. The theme survives as a
capex-order-flow idea, not as a supply-shortage one.

The sceptic also caught the researcher out on facts: zirconium sponge was
flagged as a probable chokepoint but NFC's Tuticorin complex has run at rated
capacity since 2009, and the FDI prohibition was described as an unpriced
binary still ahead when the Atomic Energy Commission had already approved an
FDI policy in April 2026.

## The finding that generalises beyond nuclear

> The Sterlite analogy inverts here. That filing worked as a signal because the
> AI data-centre demand shock was sudden and unanticipated. Indian nuclear is
> slow and heavily pre-announced, so filings arrive as confirmation of a
> programme everyone already knows about.

This is a constraint on the detector itself, not just on this theme. A filing
predicts a re-rating when it carries genuine surprise. In a twenty-year
pre-announced government programme it carries almost none, and MoUs and
"exploring opportunities in" language will be abundant and near-worthless.

Detection should therefore favour licences actually issued, site allocations
converted, and hard orders naming a counterparty and a value -- and should
suppress intent language. That distinction needs testing across every theme,
not assumed.
